WCAG 2.2 EXPLAINED

The standard, in plain English.

WCAG 2.2 is the W3C's web accessibility standard — 86 success criteria across four principles. Level AA is the legal target referenced by ADA guidance, the European Accessibility Act, and Section 508.

Every criterion falls under one of four principles, known as POUR. Hit all four and your store works for every shopper — including the screen reader user, the keyboard-only user, the colorblind user, and Google's crawler.

  • P

    Perceivable

    Alt text, captions, color contrast — content users can see or hear.
  • O

    Operable

    Full keyboard navigation, no traps, generous tap targets.
  • U

    Understandable

    Clear labels, predictable patterns, helpful error messages.
  • R

    Robust

    Semantic HTML and ARIA so assistive tech reads it correctly.
AA
Target Level AA. Level A is the floor (and not enough). Level AAA is aspirational and unachievable for most ecommerce content. Every law and settlement points to AA.

Is an accessibility overlay enough?

What is WCAG 2.2 Level AA?
WCAG 2.2 is the W3C's web accessibility standard: 86 success criteria organized under four principles — Perceivable, Operable, Understandable, Robust (POUR). Level AA is the middle of three conformance levels and is the target referenced by ADA guidance, the European Accessibility Act, and Section 508. Level A alone isn't enough, and Level AAA is aspirational and largely unachievable for ecommerce content.
Does the free check prove I am compliant?
No, and be wary of any tool that claims it does. Automated testing — including our free check — catches roughly a third to a half of accessibility barriers. It's a fast way to surface obvious issues, but full WCAG conformance also requires manual testing with a keyboard and a screen reader, which is what our paid audits and remediation include.
